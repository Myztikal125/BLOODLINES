# Summary
This research pertains to the progression and spell systems for the wizard class within BLOODLINES. The wizard class should allow for nuanced development paths and a diverse array of magical abilities, reflecting the depth and versatility associated with traditional wizard archetypes in RPGs.

# Existing Systems
1. **Dungeons & Dragons (D&D)**
   - Wizards use a spell slot system where spells are prepared ahead of time from a spellbook.
   - Features include specialization through subclasses (e.g., School of Evocation, School of Illusion).

2. **Pathfinder**
   - Similar to D&D but offers a more complex set of options, including various feats and spell metamagic that enhances spellcasting.
   - Strong emphasis on a diverse spell list and tactical use of spells.

3. **Final Fantasy Tactics**
   - Spellcasting class with a unique job system, allowing for the equipping of abilities from different classes.
   - Includes elemental interactions and combination effects based on environmental factors.

4. **World of Darkness (Mage: The Ascension)**
   - Magic defined by spheres of influence allowing for creative, narrative-driven spell effects and outcomes.
   - Focuses on the concept of spellcasting as a flexible and improvisational skill.

# Important Mechanics
- **Spell Slots and Preparation**: The balance between prepared spells and available slots, encouraging strategic choices.
- **Progression System**: Levels in spells known, or the ability to acquire higher tier spells with experience points.
- **Spell Versatility**: Ability to modify spells (via metamagic, material components, or enhancing effects).
- **School Specialization**: Allows for focused power and expertise in particular types of magic affecting spell effectiveness and gameplay style.
- **Environmental Interactions**: Incorporating environmental factors that can enhance or hinder spell capabilities.

# Strengths
- Encourages strategic planning and resource management within gameplay.
- Offers players the ability to customize their playstyle through branching progression paths.
- Can facilitate strong role-playing elements, such as the wizard's connection to lore and the arcane.

# Weaknesses
- Complexity can lead to confusion among players, especially with numerous spells and mechanics.
- Potential for imbalance if certain spells or builds dominate play, requiring careful tuning to maintain fairness.
- Reliance on external materials (e.g., spellbooks) can slow gameplay if not integrated smoothly.

# BLOODLINES Ideas
- **Signature Spells**: Allow wizards to select a few signature spells that they can cast without spending spell slots.
- **Spellcrafting System**: Introduce a mechanic whereby players can combine spells or modify existing ones with unique ingredients or rituals.
- **Loyalty to Schools**: Implement a system whereby wizards can gain bonuses from sticking to spells within their chosen school, granting them specialized abilities at various levels.
- **Arcane Tutor System**: Create opportunities for wizards to seek mentorship from NPCs to gain rare spells or exclusive knowledge.

# Implementation Notes
- Focus on user-friendly interfaces to ease understanding of spell selection and preparation.
- Develop a well-structured tutorial to introduce complex mechanics gradually to players.
- Ensure playtesting emphasizes the balance of spell effectiveness across the different schools and character builds.
- Consider a dynamic spell economy where spells may have additional costs (e.g., material components) to keep resource management engaging.

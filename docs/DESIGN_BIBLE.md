# BLOODLINES Design Bible

This document contains the official approved design decisions for BLOODLINES.

## Core Vision

BLOODLINES is an AI-assisted tabletop RPG system designed around:
- deep character identity
- meaningful choices
- evolving worlds
- strategic combat
- immersive storytelling

## Design Principles

### Player Agency
Player choices should permanently affect the world.

### Character Identity
Classes, backgrounds, and bloodlines should create unique play styles.

### Balanced Complexity
Systems should provide depth without becoming confusing.

### Living World
NPCs, factions, and locations should remember player actions.

# New Design Review

# Design Decision
The wizard class in BLOODLINES should embody the classic archetype of a wise and powerful spellcaster, with a focus on strategic depth, player customization, and narrative-driven magic. The system should balance complexity with accessibility, ensuring that new players can engage without feeling overwhelmed while offering depth for veterans.

# Approved Concepts
1. **Signature Spells**: Wizards can choose a limited number of signature spells that can be cast without expending spell slots, enhancing their identity and tactical options.
2. **Spellcrafting System**: Players can combine existing spells or modify them through unique ingredients and rituals, allowing for creativity and individual expression in gameplay.
3. **Loyalty to Schools**: Implement bonuses for wizards who focus on spells from their chosen school, creating a sense of progression and specialization.
4. **Arcane Tutor System**: Introduce NPC mentors who provide unique spells and knowledge, enhancing role-playing opportunities and expanding the spell repertoire.
5. **Dynamic Spell Economy**: Implement additional costs for spells (e.g., material components) to promote resource management and strategic decision-making.

# Rejected Concepts
1. **Overly Complex Spell Systems**: Avoid implementing excessive mechanics that could confuse players. Simplified mechanics for beginners should be embraced, rather than introducing too many overlapping systems.
2. **Environmental Factors as Major Mechanics**: While interactions should be present, they will not dominate the spellcasting experience to prevent the core systems from becoming convoluted.

# Required Changes
1. Streamline the spell selection and preparation interface to make it more user-friendly, ensuring that even new players can navigate it with ease.
2. Develop a structured tutorial that gradually introduces spellcasting mechanics, tailoring complexity to player experience levels.
3. Balance the effectiveness of spells across schools during playtesting to prevent overpowered builds and maintain fairness.

# Implementation Guidance
- Utilize visual cues and tooltips in the interface to clarify spell options and effects, minimizing the learning curve.
- Incorporate feedback loops during playtesting to adjust difficulty and complexity based on player experience.
- Emphasize lore and storytelling elements in tutorial content to enhance immersion and engagement with the wizard class.

# Future Questions
1. What specific themes of wizardry do we want to emphasize within the narrative (e.g., scholarly pursuits, chaotic experimentation, or ancient wisdom)?
2. Should there be distinct mechanics or bonuses for different schools beyond their signature spells, perhaps in their interactions with the overarching world of BLOODLINES?
3. Are we considering a tiered complexity system, where certain spells have an advanced option that experienced players can unlock later?
4. How much focus should we put on environmental interactions, and what unique elements can we incorporate to make this aspect engaging without being overly complex? 
5. Should there be set limits on spell customization in the Spellcrafting System to maintain balance, or do we want to embrace chaos in spell creation?
